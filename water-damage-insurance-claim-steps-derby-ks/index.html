Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps for Derby, KS Homes

Claims go more smoothly when you work in a clear order. This is the sequence most Derby homeowners follow, from the first minute to the final repair.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

A water damage insurance claim has a few predictable stages, and doing them in order protects both your home and your paperwork. First stop the water, then document it, then tell your insurer, then dry the structure, then rebuild. This guide walks through each stage for a Derby, KS home. It is general information, and your policy and insurer set the actual rules.

Step 1: Stop the water and make the area safe

Close the main shutoff or the fixture valve. If the water touches outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911. Safety comes before photos and phone calls.

If you are unsure where the shutoff is, find it now, before you need it. Most homes have a main valve near the water meter or where the line enters the house. Our burst pipe page explains what to do when a line fails.

Step 2: Take photos and video before you move things

Photograph the water, the source and every wet room, with wide shots and close ups. Add a short video that walks through the space. Note the date and time, and write down what you saw and what you did.

Do not throw away damaged items until your insurer has seen photos or told you it is fine. For items that are clearly unsafe, such as soaked food, photograph them first.

Step 3: Call your insurer or agent

Most policies require prompt notice. Ask how to open a claim, whether you will get a claim number, and what emergency steps they expect. Write down the name of the person you spoke with and the time of the call.

Ask whether your policy covers additional living expenses if you cannot stay in the house. Kansas Insurance Department guidance says not all policies do.

Step 4: Start mitigation right away

Mitigation means stopping more damage. It includes water extraction, removing wet materials that cannot dry and setting drying equipment. Many insurers expect you to take reasonable emergency steps, so do not wait for an adjuster to start. Our water extraction page explains the first stage.

Keep every receipt. Ask the mitigation company for photos, moisture readings and drying logs, because those support your timeline.

Step 5: Meet the adjuster

An adjuster is the insurer's representative who inspects the loss. Walk the house together, show your photos and point out every wet area. Ask when you can expect a written decision.

If your estimate and the adjuster's estimate differ, ask for the reasons in writing. You can request a review and you can ask the Kansas Insurance Department for help at 800-432-2484.

Step 6: Confirm the structure is dry

Professional drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard, which is why a dated log of readings carries weight.

Before any repair, moisture readings should show the structure is dry. Closing walls over wet framing is how hidden damage returns. A drying log with dated readings is the best proof. See structural drying and dehumidification for how it works.

Step 7: Repair and close the claim

Once the structure is dry, repair scope covers drywall, flooring, paint and trim. Get the scope in writing and compare it with the insurer's estimate. Ask whether a permit is needed before work starts, since the City of Derby lists building, electrical, mechanical and plumbing permits.

How to keep a claim file

Use one folder, paper or digital, for the whole claim. Put the claim number on the first page. Add dated photos, the adjuster's name and phone number, every estimate, every receipt and a log of calls. Save emails and text messages, and send a short follow up email after any phone call so there is a written record of what was said.

Back the folder up outside the house. A second copy in cloud storage or with a family member protects you if the loss damages the house itself.

Working with a mitigation company and your insurer

A mitigation company handles the water, not the coverage. It can give you moisture readings, photos and a drying log, and it can explain what it removed and why. Ask for these records before the job ends.

Your insurer may ask for them, and having them in hand shortens the back and forth. If the insurer wants a different approach, ask for the request in writing so everyone is working from the same plan.

When more than one policy is involved

Sometimes a loss involves a homeowners policy, a flood policy, and a warranty on an appliance or a plumbing repair. Ask each carrier or provider what it covers and in what order. A home warranty company, for example, may cover the failed appliance but not the water damage it caused.

Keep separate notes for each, so that the claim numbers and adjusters do not get mixed up.

Derby paperwork to remember

The City of Derby permits desk can be reached at 788-6632, option 2, and the main line is 316-788-1519. If a repair involves plumbing or electrical work, ask whether a permit is required before a contractor starts. Keep any permit and inspection records with your claim file, because an insurer or a future buyer may ask for them.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not sign a contract before you know the scope and price. Do not discard damaged items before documenting them. Do not close walls over wet materials. Do not assume a verbal promise from anyone means coverage, because only the insurer can confirm it in writing.

If you live near Derby

The steps are the same in Wichita and Clearwater, although permits go through different offices. Ask your city which office handles your address.

Common questions

How soon should I report water damage to my insurer?

As soon as it is safe to do so. Most policies expect prompt notice, so call or use your insurer's claim line the same day if you can.

Should I start cleanup before the adjuster arrives?

Start reasonable emergency steps right away, such as stopping the water and drying, and document everything first. Ask your insurer what it expects.

What records should I keep for a Derby claim?

Keep photos, videos, receipts, moisture readings, drying logs and notes about every call. Store a copy outside the house.

Who can help if my claim is denied?

Ask your insurer for the reason in writing, then contact the Kansas Insurance Department Consumer Assistance line at 800-432-2484.
