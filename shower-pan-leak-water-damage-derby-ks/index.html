Shower Pan Leak Water Damage: A Derby, KS Homeowner Guide

A shower pan leak is quiet. By the time a ceiling stains below, the subfloor may already be wet. Here is how to spot it and what to do.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

A shower pan is the waterproof base under a tiled shower. If it fails, water escapes into the subfloor, the walls and the room below, often for weeks before anyone sees it. The damage is hidden, which is why shower pan leaks are expensive when found late and manageable when found early. This guide covers the signs, the first steps and how the drying work is done.

Signs of a shower pan leak

Look for a few patterns. Tile grout that keeps cracking or darkening, a musty smell near the bathroom, a soft or spongy floor at the shower edge, paint bubbling on the wall outside the shower, and a stain on the ceiling of the room below. If the shower is on a ground floor over a crawl space, check underneath for dripping or damp framing.

One sign alone can have other causes. Several together point to a leak.

Simple checks you can do

Run the shower for ten minutes and watch the ceiling below. Check the drain for a loose ring or cracked seal. Look at caulk lines for gaps. A plumber can run a flood test by plugging the drain and filling the pan, which shows whether the pan holds water.

Do not cut into walls or floors yourself unless you are ready to deal with what you find. Open walls can spread dust and wet insulation.

First steps when you suspect a leak

Stop using the shower. Photograph any stains and soft spots, and note the date. If water is visibly dripping through a ceiling, place a bucket and move belongings away. Call a plumber to find and fix the source, and call a restoration company to check how far the moisture has spread.

The ceiling water damage repair page explains what happens when the leak has already reached the room below.

How moisture spreads

Water follows gravity and framing. It travels along joists, soaks insulation and drywall, and can reach a ceiling several feet from the shower. A leak that looks small at the surface can wet a large area inside the wall. Moisture meters and thermal imaging let a technician map where water is.

Plan on drying every wet layer, not only the visible stain. Our structural drying page describes the equipment.

Repair order: fix the leak, then dry, then rebuild

First fix the source. A plumber or tile contractor repairs the pan and drain. Then dry the structure until readings are normal. Only then should drywall, flooring and tile be rebuilt. Repairing finishes over wet framing invites repeat damage.

Is it covered by insurance?

Coverage depends on how the leak started. A sudden pan failure may be treated differently from a slow leak that went on for months, and many policies exclude long term seepage. Ask your insurer, and keep photos and a timeline. Insurers differ, so confirm the wording with yours.

Why tile showers fail

A tile shower depends on a waterproof layer under the tile. Over years, a pan liner can crack at corners, a drain flange can loosen and grout can open up. Settling in an older house can move the framing just enough to break the seal. Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own, so they only protect the layer beneath them as long as that layer stays intact.

Prevention that is worth doing

Check caulk lines twice a year and renew them when they crack. Replace cracked or missing grout promptly. Keep the drain clear so water does not back up in the pan. If you remodel, ask the installer what waterproofing method they use and keep the paperwork. A flood test before tile goes down is a small cost compared with opening a finished shower later.

What to do about repairs: hiring the right trades

A shower pan repair often involves a plumber for the drain and a tile setter for the pan and tile. A restoration crew handles drying. Professional drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, so ask the restoration crew to log readings. Ask the tile setter what waterproofing system they use, and ask for the product name in writing. Ask the plumber to test the drain. Schedule repairs after drying readings are normal.

Shower leaks in Derby homes

In any bathroom, the subfloor may be plywood or boards that soften when wet, so a pan leak can weaken the floor around the shower.

If the floor feels bouncy at the edge of the shower, stop using it and have it checked. The City of Derby lists plumbing permits, so ask the permits desk at 788-6632, option 2, whether your repair needs one.

What a professional looks at

A restoration technician checks moisture readings around the shower, in the floor and on the ceiling below. The crew may remove a section of wet drywall or flooring to expose framing and set air movers and dehumidifiers. The aim is to dry what can be dried and remove only what cannot.

When it is more than a pan

Sometimes the cause is a supply line behind the wall or a drain pipe, not the pan. If the water source is a pressurized line, shut off the main and call for emergency help. See burst pipe water damage for that case.

Common questions

How do I know if my shower pan is leaking?

Look for cracked grout, a musty smell, a soft floor at the shower edge, bubbling paint and stains on the ceiling below. Several signs together suggest a leak.

Can a shower pan leak damage the ceiling below?

Yes. Water travels along framing and can stain or soak the ceiling in the room under the bathroom.

Do I need a permit to repair a shower in Derby?

It can depend on the work. The City of Derby lists plumbing permits, so call the permits desk at 788-6632, option 2, before work starts.

Should I keep using a shower I think is leaking?

No. Stop using it, photograph the signs and have the source found before more water gets in.
