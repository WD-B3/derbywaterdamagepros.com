Clearwater and its river

The Ninnescah River runs west and south of Clearwater, and the town takes its name from it. The name is said to come from an Osage word for water. Clearwater was settled in 1870, platted in 1872 and incorporated in 1885, and the Chisholm Trail ran along its eastern side from 1867 to 1871. Elevation is about 1,286 feet.

Practical tie-back: living close to a river means rain and runoff are a more real concern than in a town farther from one. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask the City of Clearwater about floodplain questions. This page does not state a flood zone for any street.

Permits and paperwork

The City of Clearwater contracts with the Metropolitan Area Building and Construction Department. Residential permits are pulled through the city, commercial permits go through MABCD directly, and every building permit requires a Certificate of Zoning Compliance. Contractor licenses are obtained through Sedgwick County Code Enforcement at 316-660-1840.

That process matters when a loss leads to rebuild work. Ask the city whether your drywall, plumbing or roofing repair needs a permit before you sign a scope.

Getting help from a longer drive

Because Clearwater lies roughly 20 miles from Derby, plan to do the first steps yourself. Shut off the water, move valuables, and place towels or buckets. Stay on the phone with dispatch if you can, and ask what to watch for while a crew drives out.

Keep your own timeline. Write down when you noticed the water, when you shut it off and when you called. These notes help dispatch plan and help your insurer later.

A note on standards and safety: ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard crews follow for water damage. Do not enter water near outlets, and do not drink or cook with well or tap water after a flood until the source is cleared.
