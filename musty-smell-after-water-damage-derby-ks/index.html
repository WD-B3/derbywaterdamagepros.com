Musty Smell After Water Damage: What to Do in Derby, KS

A lingering smell usually means something is still damp. Here is how to find it and what to do about it.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

A musty smell after a water loss is a clue that moisture remains in the building. It can come from damp drywall, a wet carpet pad, a damp crawl space or insulation that never fully dried. Masking the smell does not solve the problem. Finding and drying the source does. This guide explains common causes, simple checks you can do and when to ask for professional help.

Why a smell lingers

Porous materials hold water long after the surface feels dry. Padding, drywall, insulation and wood framing can stay damp for days. Damp materials give off odors as they sit. Warm, humid air makes it worse, especially in closed rooms.

A smell can also come from the original water. Sewage and floodwater leave odors that need cleaning, not just drying. See sewage backup cleanup for that case.

Simple checks

Check for cool, damp spots on walls and floors. Lift a corner of carpet and feel the pad. Look at baseboards and the bottom of walls for staining or swelling. Sniff near closets and under sinks. Open a crawl space hatch and look for damp soil or wet framing.

A hygrometer measures indoor humidity. A reading that stays above about 60 percent suggests the air is too damp. Dehumidifiers help, but they do not replace finding the source.

Hidden moisture

Water can sit behind baseboards, inside wall cavities and under flooring. A technician uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to locate it. Do not tear into walls blindly, since you may spread dust and miss the wet area.

Our wall and drywall repair page explains how wet walls are assessed.

Carpet and flooring

Wet carpet padding is a frequent culprit. If padding stayed wet, removal is often the fix. Hardwood that was never dried properly can trap moisture below the surface. See carpet restoration and hardwood floor repair.

What not to do

Do not rely on air fresheners or scented products to cover the smell. Do not seal or paint over damp materials. Do not run a heater at high temperature in a closed room. These can trap moisture.

Humidity and ventilation

Indoor humidity above about 60 percent can keep a house smelling damp even after the water is gone. Run a dehumidifier in the affected area and empty it often. Use bathroom and kitchen fans while cooking and showering. Open windows on dry days if outdoor air is drier than indoor air.

Keep furniture a few inches from walls to let air move. Avoid piling boxes against exterior walls in a basement.

Cleaning that helps

Hard surfaces can be washed with detergent and warm water. Fabrics can be laundered. Carpets that smell after drying may need deeper cleaning or replacement of the pad. Activated charcoal or baking soda can absorb some odors, but they are helpers, not a fix for a wet source.

If a smell returns after cleaning, something is still damp. Ask a technician to check moisture again.

Which rooms to check first

Start where the water was, then check the rooms below and beside it. Closets, under sinks, behind furniture and inside cabinets hold moisture longest because air does not move there. In a basement, check the corners and the base of walls. In a crawl space, check the vapor barrier and the soil for wet spots.

Write down where the smell is strongest. A technician can use that clue with moisture readings to find the source.

When the smell is not from water damage

Sometimes a smell has another cause, such as a dirty drain trap, a dry floor drain, an old appliance or a pest. Pour water into rarely used drains to refill traps. Clean the washing machine and dishwasher according to their manuals. If the smell has a sharp or gassy character, leave the house and call the utility, because that is a safety question and not a moisture one.

Smells in Derby homes

Many Derby homes have basements or crawl spaces, and spring and summer storms can leave those areas damp for days. If a smell is strongest near a lower level, check for groundwater and for a failed sump pump.

Haysville, Mulvane and Clearwater homes near creeks and rivers have similar issues. If you think a smell comes from mold, note that this site does not offer mold removal, and your health care provider or a certified specialist can advise on that.

Drying done right

Professional drying uses air movers and dehumidifiers and tracks readings daily until the structure reaches a normal level. A lingering smell after drying may mean something was missed. Ask for the moisture log and ask the crew to recheck.

When to call for help

Call when the smell lasts more than a few days, when it comes with visible staining or when you cannot find the source. A moisture check is a reasonable first step. Our water extraction crews can also pull out standing water that you cannot see.

Common questions

Why does my house smell musty after water damage?

Something is probably still damp, such as padding, drywall, insulation or a crawl space. Finding and drying the source is the fix.

Will a dehumidifier get rid of the smell?

It helps reduce humidity, but it does not remove wet materials. Find the source too.

How long should a house smell after drying?

A properly dried house should not keep a musty smell. If it does, ask for a recheck of readings.

Does this company remove mold?

No. This site covers water damage drying and cleanup, not mold removal.
