Water Damage in a Rental: Tenants and Landlords in Derby, KS

When water gets into a rental, speed and written notice protect everyone. Here is a plain guide for tenants and landlords.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

When water gets into a rental, two people have to act quickly: the tenant, who sees it first, and the landlord, who controls the building. Delay costs both of them. This guide covers the usual steps for each side, the records to keep and the questions to ask.

It is general information. Kansas has a Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, and a lease may add terms, so check both or ask an attorney.

What tenants should do first

Stop the water if you can, such as by closing a fixture shutoff. Stay out of water near outlets. Take photos and video of the water and the damage. Then notify the landlord or property manager right away, and put it in writing by text or email as well as by phone, so there is a record.

Move your belongings to a dry place if it is safe. Keep receipts for any emergency costs.

What landlords should do first

Respond promptly, shut off the source and call a plumber or restoration crew. Water in one unit often reaches the unit below, so check neighbors. A multi-unit water damage plan can cover several units at once.

Document the loss, notify your insurer and keep tenants informed about timing.

Who pays for what

Generally, a landlord's insurance covers the building, and a tenant's renter's insurance covers the tenant's belongings and, in some policies, temporary housing. A tenant is not automatically responsible for damage they did not cause. If a tenant caused it, such as by leaving a tub running, the lease and the facts decide.

Ask each insurer what is covered. Renters and landlords each have their own policy.

Renters insurance is worth having

Renters insurance often costs little compared with the value of belongings. It may cover the loss of furniture and electronics and may help with temporary housing. Ask your insurer whether sewer backup is included, since Kansas Insurance Department guidance says not all policies cover it.

Moving out or staying

If a unit is not livable, the lease and Kansas law decide what happens to rent. Ask an attorney or a tenant resource if you need advice. Keep written notice of the problem and any response. Do not stop paying rent without advice.

Drying and repairs

Professional drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, so ask the restoration company for the readings that show each unit reached its drying goal.

The landlord arranges drying and repairs. Ask for the schedule and for access dates. Drying equipment runs for days, and it is loud and warm. Ask when it will be removed.

See structural drying for how long the process usually lasts.

A sample timeline

On day one, the tenant reports the leak by phone and by text. The landlord shuts off the source and calls a plumber and a restoration crew the same day. Over the next several days, equipment dries the unit and neighbors are checked. After the structure is dry, repair work is scheduled, and the tenant is told when access will be needed.

Keeping each step in writing helps if there is a later dispute about timing or cost.

Tips for property managers

Keep a list of emergency contacts and shutoff locations for each building. Post the main valve location in a place tenants can find. Train staff to check ceilings below bathrooms when a unit reports a leak. After every loss, record the cause and the fix, because patterns point to aging pipes or recurring appliance failures.

Ask your restoration crew for a written report for each unit. It makes insurance paperwork faster and gives tenants a clear explanation.

Common sources in rentals

Upstairs bathrooms, water heaters in utility closets, washing machine hoses and air conditioner condensate lines cause most rental losses. In older buildings, shared drain stacks and roof leaks add risk. Water that appears in a lower unit often started in the unit above, so a landlord should check upstairs neighbors when a ceiling stains.

Tenants can help by reporting drips early and by not ignoring a small stain.

When a tenant caused the loss

If a tenant left a tub running, a washing machine hose unattached or a window open in a storm, the lease and the facts decide who pays. A tenant's liability coverage under a renters policy may respond. Landlords should document the cause and communicate calmly in writing.

Rental notes for Derby

Derby has a large rental market, including housing near McConnell Air Force Base where tenants may move on military timelines. If you are leaving soon, tell the landlord about any water damage in writing before you move out, and take dated photos of the unit's condition. The City of Derby main line is 316-788-1519 if you need to ask which office handles housing or code complaints.

Mold and odors in rentals

Persistent moisture can cause odors. Report them promptly. A damp smell usually means moisture is still present. This site does not offer mold removal.

Records both sides should keep

Keep the lease, dated photos, written notices, repair invoices and moisture logs. Tenants should keep renters insurance papers. Landlords should keep insurer correspondence.

Common questions

Who pays for water damage in a rental in Kansas?

It depends on the cause and the lease. The landlord's insurance generally covers the building and the tenant's renters insurance covers the tenant's belongings.

Should a tenant notify the landlord in writing?

Yes. Call, then send a text or email so there is a written record with a date.

Can I stop paying rent after a leak?

Do not stop without advice. Ask an attorney or tenant resource, because rules and lease terms vary.

Does renters insurance cover a pipe burst?

Often it covers your belongings from sudden events, but policies differ. Ask your insurer.
