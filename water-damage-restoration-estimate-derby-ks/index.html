What a Water Damage Restoration Estimate Should Include in Derby, KS

A good estimate lists what will be done, how it will be measured and what is not included. Here is a checklist for Derby homeowners.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

A water damage restoration estimate should tell you what will be done, how long it will take, what equipment will be used and what is not included. If an estimate is a single number with no detail, you cannot tell what you are paying for.

This guide lists the items a clear estimate should have, so you can compare quotes and ask better questions. It does not quote prices, because every loss is different.

Scope of work

The scope lists the tasks: water extraction, removal of wet materials, cleaning, drying and any treatment of contaminated areas. It should name the rooms and the approximate area affected. Each line should say what is done, where and how much.

Ask for the scope in writing before work begins.

Water category and class

A professional estimate names the water category, from clean to grossly contaminated, and the class of the loss, which describes how much of the structure is wet. These come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard and shape the plan. A sewage loss costs differently from a clean supply line loss, and the estimate should say which one it is.

Equipment and drying days

Look for the number of air movers and dehumidifiers and the expected days in place. Equipment counts depend on the size of the wet area. Ask how readings will be taken and how you will know the structure is dry. The structural drying page explains the process.

Materials removed

If drywall, insulation, padding or flooring will be removed, the estimate should say so and give the extent. Ask what the cut lines will be and how disposal is handled. Photos before removal protect you in an insurance review.

Labor, disposal and other charges

The estimate should show labor, equipment, materials and disposal in some form. Ask about minimums, after hours charges and travel charges. A clear estimate says what triggers a change in price.

What is excluded

Every estimate has exclusions. Common ones are rebuild work, contents cleaning, mold treatment and permit fees. Ask which items could be added later and how a change would be approved. Do not accept a verbal promise in place of a written line.

Insurance and estimates

Your insurer will usually write its own estimate. The two may not match, because insurers use their own pricing tools. Share the restoration scope and the moisture logs with the adjuster, and ask for reasons if numbers differ. Coverage decisions belong to the insurer, and the order of steps matters more than the speed of any one of them.

Line items you may see

Estimates often list extraction by the hour or by the square foot, equipment by the day, and demolition by the square foot. You may see charges for containment, protective sheeting and air scrubbing for dirty water. You may see disposal fees and a line for monitoring visits. None of these is unusual, but each should be tied to a task.

If a line says miscellaneous, ask what it covers. A small allowance for supplies is normal. A large unexplained amount is not.

How to ask for changes

If you disagree with part of the scope, say so before work starts. Ask the contractor to revise the estimate, and wait for the new version. Do not rely on a verbal agreement. If work has already started, ask for a written change order before the next step.

Keep every version of the estimate. When your insurer asks for documents, you will have a clear record of what was proposed and what was approved.

Timing and updates

A good estimate states how long the job should take and when readings will be checked. It explains what would extend the schedule, such as a hidden wet area behind a wall. Ask for a plain update each day during drying, even a short text. Clear communication is part of what you are paying for.

Derby estimate details

Derby estimates should also mention permits when the work needs them. The City of Derby lists building, electrical, mechanical, plumbing and flood plain permits, and the permits desk is at 788-6632, option 2. If the scope includes opening walls to repair plumbing or wiring, ask who pulls the permit and who pays the fee. If your property is in a flood plain, ask the city how that affects repairs.

Red flags in an estimate

Be cautious about a quote with no itemization, a request for full payment up front, or a promise to handle the insurance claim for you. Be cautious about any company that guarantees an arrival time or tells you that insurance will definitely pay. Good companies explain scope and price before work starts and let you ask questions.

How to compare two estimates

Put the estimates side by side. Check that both cover the same rooms, the same materials removed and the same drying days. Ask each company to explain any difference. The lowest number is not always the best, because a quote that leaves out tasks may grow later.

Free of surprises

Ask what would change the price. A hidden source of water, a larger wet area than first seen or contaminated materials can add tasks. A good company tells you before it adds them.

Nearby towns

Estimates in Haysville or Mulvane should include the same items, though permits go through different offices.

Common questions

What should a water damage estimate include?

It should list scope, water category, equipment, drying days, materials removed, labor and disposal, and what is excluded.

Why do two estimates for the same loss differ?

They may cover different tasks, equipment counts or materials removed. Ask each company to itemize and explain.

Should I pay in full before work starts?

Be cautious about requests for full payment up front. Ask for a written scope and payment terms before you agree.

Does the estimate include rebuild work?

Not always. Many estimates cover mitigation only, so ask whether repair and permits are included.
