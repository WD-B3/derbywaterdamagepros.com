Water Damaged Electronics: When to Unplug in Derby, KS

Before you touch anything wet, check the power. This guide explains what is safe, what is not and what to do with wet devices.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

The first rule after a water loss is that people come before devices. If water is touching outlets, cords, appliances or the electrical panel, do not walk through it and do not reach for plugs.

Cutting power at a dry panel is safe in many cases, but if the panel itself is wet, call the utility or 911. This guide explains the safe order of steps and what to do with the electronics that got wet.

Check the water before you check the device

Look at where the water is. Standing water around an outlet means an electrical risk. If the floor is wet and you cannot reach the panel without stepping through water, stay out. The utility can shut off power at the meter or the pole.

If the area is dry and you can reach the panel safely, turn off the breakers for wet rooms. Then you can unplug items from dry outlets with dry hands. If you are unsure, wait for a professional.

Unplugging safely

Only unplug devices when the cord, the outlet and the floor are dry and the breaker is off. Never touch a plugged in cord while standing in water. Never use a wet extension cord or power strip, and discard any that got wet.

Do not turn on a wet device to see if it works. Power can short a circuit board that would otherwise have been saved.

What to do with devices that got wet

Once a device is unplugged, remove batteries and any removable parts. Wipe away surface water with a dry cloth, and move the device to a dry, airy place. Do not use heat to speed up drying, and do not shake it. Take it to a repair shop as soon as you can, because corrosion begins within hours.

Rice does not reliably dry electronics. Professional cleaning with proper solvents is more reliable for valuable devices.

Appliances and wiring

Large appliances, such as refrigerators, washers and water heaters, are not safe to run after flooding until a technician checks them. Wet wiring inside walls may need to be inspected. Our appliance leak page explains what happens when an appliance is the source.

Ask a licensed electrician to inspect any outlets and wiring that were submerged or soaked. The City of Derby lists electrical permits, so ask whether replacement work needs one.

Document the damage

Photograph each device, its serial number and the damage before you move it. List the age and approximate value, since your insurer may ask. Keep damaged devices until your insurer has had a chance to see them.

When the water is contaminated

ANSI/IICRC S500 classes sewage and floodwater as Category 3, which is why devices that met it are treated as contaminated.

If the water was sewage or floodwater, treat devices as contaminated. Cleaning them is a job for a professional, and some items are not worth saving. See flood damage restoration for how contaminated water is handled.

Computers, phones and tablets

If a laptop or phone got wet, power it off, unplug the charger and remove the battery if you can. Do not press buttons to test it. Wipe the outside with a dry cloth and let it sit in a dry, airy place. Take it to a repair shop soon, since corrosion builds as moisture sits inside.

Desktop computers, game consoles and televisions can hold charge after they are unplugged. Leave them alone until a technician has checked them. Back up data from an undamaged drive as soon as you can.

Cords, strips and small appliances

Throw away any power strip, extension cord or surge protector that got wet. The insulation can fail even when the cord looks fine. Small kitchen appliances such as coffee makers and toasters should be dried thoroughly and checked before use. When in doubt, replace a cheap appliance rather than gamble on it.

Fans and space heaters that sat in water should not be plugged in again until they have been checked. Replacement is often the safest choice.

Home offices and entertainment centers

Home offices and entertainment centers hold many devices on one power strip. If water reaches the strip, cut the power at the breaker first. Then unplug devices one by one with dry hands, and take photos of each. Back up what you can from drives that were not wet.

Take a short inventory with model numbers while everything is in front of you.

Power and water in Derby homes

Many homes have outlets near the floor in basements and garages. Those outlets are the first to be reached by water.

If a storm or a plumbing failure floods a lower level, assume the outlets there are unsafe until checked. The City of Derby main line is 316-788-1519 if you need to ask which department can help with a permit or inspection.

Smart devices and chargers

Wi-Fi routers, smart speakers and chargers plugged into low outlets are easy to forget. Unplug these when it is safe, and replace any charger or adapter that got wet. A wet charger can fail with a short, and it is not worth the risk.

After the water is gone

When the structure is dry, an electrician can test circuits before you restore power to affected rooms. Drying equipment draws power too, so a crew will plan which circuits to use. See structural drying and dehumidification for how equipment is placed.

Common questions

Is it safe to unplug electronics in a flooded room?

Only if the cord, outlet and floor are dry and the breaker is off. If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911.

Can I turn on a wet device to see if it works?

No. Powering on a wet device can short it and cause more damage. Unplug it and have it checked.

Does putting a phone in rice work?

Not reliably. Remove the battery if you can, power it off and take it to a repair shop soon.

Do I need an electrician after a flood in Derby?

A licensed electrician should inspect soaked wiring and outlets, and the City of Derby lists electrical permits for rebuild work.
