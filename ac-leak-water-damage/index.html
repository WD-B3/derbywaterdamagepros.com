AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Derby, KS

Most AC water damage comes from a clogged condensate line or a full drain pan. Turn the system off at the thermostat and call.

AC leak water damage is water released by a central or split air conditioning system, almost always from the condensate drain, a cracked drain pan or a frozen evaporator coil that thaws. In Derby, KS, many systems sit in attics or closets above finished ceilings, so a clogged line can soak drywall for days before a stain shows.

The water is usually clean, but standing condensate in a pan can grow slime and carry an odor.

Turn the thermostat to off so the system stops producing more condensate, and set a bucket under any drip. If water is near the air handler's wiring, leave it alone and call a licensed HVAC technician. Call us once the leak is contained and wet materials need attention.

When an AC leak calls for a crew

An AC leak is not always an emergency, but a ceiling that is dripping or sagging is. If you also have a stain below the unit, read the ceiling page and call dispatch the same hour. The line is open 24/7 for any urgent call.

Not every AC leak is urgent

A small drip into a pan that you can empty and clean is a maintenance issue. Wet drywall, a stained ceiling or a soft closet floor means water has left the system and needs drying.

Why central AC leaks happen

Condensate drains collect algae and dust, and the line clogs over a hot summer. When it does, the pan fills and overflows, or a safety float switch fails to cut the unit off. A frozen coil is another route, because ice melts into the pan faster than it can drain. A technician can flush the line and test the float switch, which is a good yearly check.

Hot, humid weather means the system runs long cycles and makes more condensate, so leaks tend to appear in the middle of a stretch of heat. A line that was dry in spring can overflow by July.

Signs of an AC leak inside the house

  • A stain near an air vent or on the ceiling below the attic unit.
  • Water in the drain pan or a rusty pan.
  • A closet floor that feels damp or smells musty.
  • An air handler that stopped cooling and has frost on the coil.
  • Warped baseboard near a wall unit or closet.

How the repair works

The crew checks moisture with a meter and a thermal camera, then removes wet insulation and drywall that cannot be saved. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the cavity, and readings are tracked until dry. The HVAC technician repairs the line or the pan separately.

Wood and laminate floors near a closet air handler can cup after repeated wetting. The hardwood floor page covers that outcome, and the structural drying page explains the drying equipment.

What affects the cost

The crew gives you scope and price before any wall is opened. The factors behind it:

  • How long the line leaked before it was caught.
  • Whether insulation and drywall are wet or just damp.
  • Flooring type in the affected rooms.
  • Drying days and equipment count.
  • Repair and paint once readings are dry.

Insurers often treat a sudden overflow differently from a slow, long-term drip, and some exclude maintenance related failures. Ask your insurer what applies. If you want a number for your house, describe the damage to dispatch.

Small habits that prevent AC leaks

Change filters on schedule, since a clogged filter can freeze the coil and cause overflow when it melts. Flush the condensate line once a year with a vinegar and water mix if the manufacturer allows it. Check the drain pan for rust and water. A float switch can shut the unit down before the pan overflows.

If the unit sits in an attic, check it before the cooling season starts. A small stain on a ceiling in spring is easier to fix than a soaked ceiling in July.

Safety around a leaking air conditioner

Turn off the thermostat and the unit's breaker, and place a bucket under any dripping ceiling. Do not use the system until the leak is found. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference for categorizing condensate water and planning the drying of ceilings and insulation.

Common questions

Is an AC leak a 24/7 emergency?

A ceiling that bulges or drips steadily is. A damp pan is not. Dispatch is open 24/7 for the first case, and the call taker will help you decide which one you have.

Who fixes the air conditioner itself?

A licensed HVAC technician repairs the line, pan and switch. The restoration crew dries and repairs what the water touched.

Can a clogged condensate line cause mold?

Standing water and damp materials give mold what it needs. Fix the source, dry everything and ask your insurer or a physician about any health concerns.

How do I prevent another AC leak?

Have a technician flush the drain line each season and confirm the float switch works. A small cup of dry filter changes also helps airflow.
