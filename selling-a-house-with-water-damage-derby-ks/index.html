Selling a House With Water Damage: Disclosure Basics, Derby KS

Water damage does not have to end a sale, but hiding it can. Here is how repair records and honest disclosure help.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

A house with a past water problem can still sell, and a well documented repair often reassures buyers. What damages a sale is a surprise, such as a stain found at inspection or a claim history the seller did not mention. This guide explains how documentation helps, why honesty protects you and which professionals to ask about the rules. It is general information and not legal advice.

Fix and document before you list

If the damage is current, dry and repair it before you list. Keep the moisture readings, photos and final drying log, which show that the structure was dried to a normal level. Keep receipts and permits for the repair. A buyer who sees a clean paper trail is more likely to believe the fix.

See structural drying and dehumidification for how readings are recorded.

Disclosure is a legal question

Rules about what a seller must tell a buyer depend on the state and the situation. In general, concealing a known defect can create legal risk, and honest disclosure protects you. Ask your real estate agent and, if needed, an attorney what applies to your sale. Do not rely on a blog to decide.

Kansas sellers commonly use a seller disclosure form through their agent, so ask your agent which form is used and what it asks about water.

What buyers and inspectors look for

Inspectors look for stains, soft spots, musty odors, signs of past repairs and high moisture readings. They may check crawl spaces, basements and under sinks. A recently painted ceiling can raise questions. If you have records, share them early.

Insurance history matters

A past claim for water damage may appear in a claims history report, which a buyer's insurer can see. That is not a reason to skip a claim you need. It is a reason to keep good records and repair work properly.

Coverage depends on your policy, so ask your insurer.

Should you repair or sell as is

Repair before listing if the cost is modest and the structure is dry. Sell as is if the damage is large, you are short on time or the buyer pool includes investors. In either case, tell the buyer what you know. Ask your agent how each choice affects price and time on the market.

Talking to buyers and their inspectors

Buyers usually hire an inspector, and the inspector may use a moisture meter. If you know of a past leak, tell your agent so the listing can say what was repaired and when. Offer the repair records and the final moisture readings. A buyer who sees clear records is more likely to relax.

If an inspector finds a problem, be ready to respond. A written plan to dry and repair, with a schedule, can keep a deal alive.

Pricing and negotiation

A known water issue can affect price. Ask your agent for comparable sales and for the effect of repairs on value. Buyers sometimes ask for a credit instead of a repair, and some lenders require repairs before closing. Your agent can explain the options for your situation.

A home with a documented, completed repair often sells on better terms than a home with an unexplained stain.

Lender and appraisal concerns

A lender's appraiser may note visible water damage and require repair before closing. Some loan programs have condition rules for the property. If damage is current, plan for repairs to be finished and documented before an appraisal. Ask your agent which loan types your likely buyers will use.

If the damage is in a rental or inherited property

Sellers who inherited a house or manage a rental may not know its water history. Ask neighbors, past tenants and the insurer for claim records, and have a moisture inspection done before listing. Telling a buyer what you do know, and what you do not, is better than staying silent.

Derby selling notes

Derby is a suburban city of about 25,625 people. Buyers in any neighborhood may ask about plumbing age, water heaters and basement or crawl space moisture.

If your home is in a mapped flood plain, ask the City of Derby, at 788-6632 option 2, about flood plain permits and records, since a buyer and a lender may ask.

Permits and records

A drying log that follows ANSI/IICRC S500 is the kind of record buyers and inspectors recognize.

If repairs needed permits, keep the permit and inspection records. Unpermitted work can slow a sale. The City of Derby permits desk can say what applies.

Mold and odors

A musty smell can cost a showing. Dry the structure fully, then address any lingering smell. Damp padding, drywall and crawl spaces are the usual causes. This site does not offer mold removal.

Questions to ask your agent

Ask which disclosure form applies, what you must state about past water damage and whether to share repair records with the listing. Ask about pricing if the damage is current. Ask what buyers in your price range expect.

Common questions

Do I have to disclose past water damage when selling in Kansas?

Rules depend on the situation, so ask your real estate agent and, if needed, an attorney. Concealing a known defect can create legal risk.

Do drying records help a sale?

They can. Moisture readings, photos and receipts show the home was dried and repaired.

Will a past claim show up for a buyer?

A claims history report may show it. Keep records and repair work properly.

Should I repair water damage before listing?

Often, if the cost is modest. Otherwise sell as is and disclose what you know.
