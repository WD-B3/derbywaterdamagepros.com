What to Keep or Throw Away After Water Damage in Derby, KS

After a water loss, the hard part is deciding what is worth saving. This guide sorts common items by what the water was and how long they stayed wet.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

The two questions that decide whether something can be saved are what kind of water it met and how long it stayed wet. Clean water from a supply line is far kinder to belongings than water from a sewer backup or a flooded yard.

Time matters too, because porous items soak up moisture quickly. This guide gives practical rules of thumb, and a restoration professional can give a firm answer for a specific item.

Start with the water category

Professionals describe water in three categories under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Category 1 is clean water from a broken supply line. Category 2 is water with some contamination, such as washing machine overflow. Category 3 is grossly contaminated water, such as sewage or floodwater from outside.

Items that touched Category 3 water are usually thrown away if they are porous, and cleaned carefully if they are hard and non porous. Our sewage backup page explains why that caution is warranted.

Items that are often worth saving

Hard, non porous items such as glass, metal, solid plastic and glazed ceramic can usually be cleaned and dried. Solid wood furniture can often be dried, though veneers and glued joints can fail. Photographs, papers and books can sometimes be dried or frozen for later work, using the steps for paper and photographs further down.

Clothing and linens that met only clean water can usually be laundered. Sort them quickly, because damp fabric starts to smell within a day or two.

Items that are often thrown away

Carpet padding that has been wet usually goes, because it holds water and is cheap to replace. Mattresses and upholstered furniture that soaked up Category 2 or 3 water are normally discarded. Particleboard furniture swells and loses its strength when wet. Food in contact with floodwater or sewage should be thrown out, and so should open food that was near contaminated water.

Drywall and insulation that stayed wet for a long time often need replacing. See drywall water damage repair for how that call is made.

A simple sorting method

Set up three areas outside the wet room. Label one Keep and Clean, one Dry First and one Discard. Photograph every item before it moves. For anything in the Discard pile, keep photos and a short list with the item, age and estimated value, because your insurer may ask for them.

Do not throw items away before you have documented them. If your insurer wants to see an item, set it aside and tell them.

Electronics need extra care

Do not plug in wet electronics. Unplug them if it is safe to do so, and let a technician or repair shop look at them. Wet electronics can look fine and still fail or short when powered.

Why speed matters

Porous items start to change within hours. Paper warps, fabric stains, leather stiffens and wood swells. Moving fast gives you the widest set of options. If you cannot deal with everything at once, move the items you care most about first and ask a professional to handle the rest.

Clothing, linens and soft goods

Launder washable fabrics as soon as you can, using the hottest water the label allows. Clothing that met clean water and dried within a day or two usually comes out fine. Dry cleaning can handle some delicate pieces. If a smell remains after washing, wash again with a laundry additive before drying, because heat can set odors.

Stuffed toys and pillows are a judgment call. If they met clean water and can go in the washer, they often survive. If they met sewage or floodwater, most families choose to replace them.

Kitchen items and food

Throw away any food that touched floodwater or sewage, along with anything in cans or jars with damaged seals. Refrigerator and freezer contents that lost power for long periods may be unsafe, and a thermometer helps you decide. Dishes, pots and glassware can usually be washed and sanitized if they are not cracked.

Wooden cutting boards and utensils that soaked in dirty water are better replaced. Metal and glass can usually be sanitized with hot water and a household sanitizer.

Derby tips for sorting after a loss

Many Derby households have storage in garages or basements that hold older family items. If water reached a basement or garage, check cardboard boxes first, because cardboard collapses and wicks moisture upward. Move boxes to a dry, well ventilated space and open them to check contents.

The Derby Historical Museum is housed in a historic school building, and local residents sometimes donate or ask about old photographs, so ask a museum or historical society before discarding historic family papers.

What a restoration crew can do for belongings

Crews focus on the building, but they can often move furniture, protect contents in place and dry them along with the room. If large amounts of contents are involved, ask about a separate contents service. Scope and price are explained before any work starts.

When to call for help

If you are unsure whether an item is safe, ask. If you smell sewage, see a visible film or notice a strong damp odor, treat items as contaminated until a professional says otherwise. A lingering odor after cleanup usually means something is still damp.

Common questions

Can I keep a sofa that got wet in Derby?

It depends on the water type and how long it stayed wet. Clean water and quick drying can save some pieces, but upholstery that met sewage or floodwater is usually thrown out.

Should I throw away wet carpet padding?

Usually yes. Padding holds water and is inexpensive to replace, so it is commonly removed even when the carpet above can be dried.

What should I photograph before throwing something away?

Photograph the item, any labels or serial numbers, and the damage. Keep a short list with the age and estimated value.

Can wet books and papers be saved?

Often, if they are handled quickly. Freezing damp papers can stop further damage until they can be dried properly.
