What a Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill Can Mean in Derby, KS

A bill that doubles with no change in habits is a clue. Here is how to test for a hidden leak and what to do if you find one.

By the Derby Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

A sudden jump in a water bill is often the first sign of a hidden leak. A running toilet, a dripping fixture or a leaking line under the slab can use thousands of gallons without leaving a puddle. The good news is that you can run a simple test in about fifteen minutes. This guide explains how, and what to do next, in plain steps.

Look at the bill first

Compare the new bill with the same month last year. Check whether the billing period was longer, whether the rate changed and whether the usage number really jumped. Guests, a new baby or filling a pool can explain a rise. If none apply, check for a leak.

Derby buys its water wholesale from the City of Wichita, but your bill comes from your own city utility office. Call that office if the reading looks wrong.

The meter test

Turn off every fixture and appliance inside and outside the house. Find your water meter, usually in a box near the street, and lift the lid. Look at the small leak indicator, often a triangle or a dial, which spins if water is flowing. Write down the reading, wait fifteen minutes with nothing running and check again. A change means water is moving somewhere.

Check the usual suspects

Toilets are the most common source. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper leaks. Check faucets, hose bibs and the water heater relief valve. Look at irrigation lines, since a broken line can run for weeks.

If these are fine and the meter still moves, the leak may be underground or in a wall. Our slab leak page explains that case.

Signs of a hidden leak

Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water when fixtures are off, damp patches in a yard, cracked paint, musty odors and low water pressure can all point to a leak. One sign is weak evidence. Several together are stronger.

What to do when you find one

Shut off the water at the main valve if you cannot stop the leak. Call a plumber to find and fix the source. If the leak has already wet floors, walls or ceilings, call a restoration crew to dry the structure. See burst pipe water damage for what that involves.

Billing adjustments

Some cities offer a one time adjustment for a leak that was repaired. Ask your city utility office whether it has such a policy, and what proof it wants. Keep the plumber's receipt and the repair date.

Reading the meter numbers

Most meters show a total reading in gallons or cubic feet, plus a small dial that moves when water flows. Write down the total at the same time each day for a few days. A steady rise when nobody is home suggests a leak. If the rise is smaller than a gallon per hour, a toilet flapper is the likely culprit.

Take a photo of the meter each time. If you later ask for a billing adjustment, dated photos are useful evidence.

Outdoor sources

Check hose bibs for drips and look at the ground around the meter for wet spots or unusually green grass. An irrigation system can leak underground without any sign inside the house. Turn the system off at the controller and recheck the meter. If the meter stops, the sprinklers are the cause.

A broken service line between the meter and the house is a repair for a plumber, and the responsibility for that segment is set by the city and your property line.

When the leak is inside a wall

A pinhole in a copper line can spray inside a wall for weeks. The sound of hissing near a wall, a damp spot that grows or paint that bubbles are warning signs. A technician can use a moisture meter and a thermal camera to trace the area without cutting. Shut off the main if you see water coming through a ceiling or wall.

After the plumber fixes the line, the wet materials still need drying. See the structural drying page for what that involves.

Derby water and your bill

Derby buys water wholesale from the City of Wichita and follows Wichita's watering restrictions, which allow watering on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays. A summer bill that spikes may be from irrigation, not a leak, so check your sprinkler timer first.

If the meter test shows flow with everything off, treat it as a leak. The City of Derby main line is 316-788-1519 and can direct you to the right utility office.

Insurance and leaks

A sudden pipe burst may be covered for the damage it causes, but slow leaks are often excluded, and the water you lost is not usually covered. Ask your insurer, and keep the plumber's invoice.

Prevention

Learn where your main shutoff is and test it once a year. Replace old washing machine hoses. Check under sinks for dampness. Consider a leak sensor near the water heater and washer.

Common questions

How do I know if I have a hidden leak?

Turn off all water, check the meter, wait fifteen minutes and check again. If the reading changed, water is moving somewhere.

Why did my Derby water bill jump?

Common causes are a leaking toilet, a broken irrigation line, a hidden pipe leak or higher summer use. Run the meter test to tell.

Will insurance pay for the water I lost?

Usually not. Coverage may apply to the sudden damage the leak caused, so ask your insurer.

Who do I call about my meter?

Call your city utility office. The City of Derby main line is 316-788-1519.
