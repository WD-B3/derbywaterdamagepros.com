Haysville homes face rain, drainage and aging plumbing. The 24/7 emergency line sends Derby crews to extract, dry and document the loss.

What kind of housing Haysville has

Haysville began when W.W. Hays, who came to the area in the early 1870s, platted land in 1891 so a town could be built. The railroad reached it in 1887, and truck farming and peaches gave the town its nickname, the Peach Capital of Kansas. In 1999 an F4 tornado destroyed the historic district and damaged about 150 homes, and much of the housing near that area was rebuilt afterward.

Practical tie-back: after the 1999 tornado, part of Haysville was rebuilt, but we have no verified data on home ages. Different plumbing fails in different ways, so a leak call should start with what the house is built with. Ask the crew to check moisture where the pipes run, not just where the stain appears.

When water is spreading in Haysville

If water is moving across a floor in 67060, close the main valve or the fixture shutoff and keep everyone out of water that reaches outlets or the panel. Then call the 24/7 emergency line. Haysville is about 8 miles west of Derby, and dispatch will tell you honestly how soon a crew can come. A same-day visit is possible when a crew is free.

Before we arrive in Haysville

Shut off the source

Find the main valve or the fixture shutoff and turn it fully off. Open a low faucet to drain the line.

Stay out of electrical risk

If the panel or outlets are wet, leave them alone and call the utility or 911 first.

Photograph and lift

Take photos before moving anything, then raise valuables onto dry furniture.

Flood and drainage in Haysville

This is where Haysville differs from most Derby-area towns. The City of Haysville says most of the city is protected by the Wichita-Valley Center Floodway, now called the MS Mitch Mitchell Floodway, and its maps show those areas as reduced flood risk due to the levee.

It also says flooding is most common north of the floodway and along Cowskin Creek, and that retention ponds and flash flooding can affect areas that do not normally flood.

Practical tie-back: if your Haysville house sits near Cowskin Creek or a retention pond, rain entry into a basement or crawl space is worth considering alongside a pipe failure. The basement water damage page covers that case.

Haysville flood rules worth knowing

The city's flood page says any development in a floodplain, including construction, filling, excavation and fences, requires a permit. It also says that if repairs or improvements, from any cause, cost more than 50 percent of a building's market value excluding land, the whole building must meet current floodplain standards. Costs are totaled over five years.

That 50 percent rule can change a restoration plan for a badly damaged building. If a loss is large, ask the Haysville Planning Department, which the city lists at 316-529-5900, before you commit to rebuild scope. Flood insurance has a 30 day waiting period, and because Haysville participates in the Community Rating System, residents can get a premium discount.

Haysville places that show up in water calls

Riggs Park hosts the Fall Festival and the Fourth of July fireworks, and the Haysville Community Library and Activity Center sit near the center of town. City Hall is at 200 W Grand. Haysville USD 261 runs Haysville High School, two middle schools and six elementary schools. A larger public building like a school or library has its own water risks, which the commercial water damage page explains.

Haysville had 11,262 residents in the 2020 Census. For households in the Wichita side of that population, Wichita has its own page, and Mulvane lies south.

ZIP coverage and cost

Haysville's ZIP is 67060, and calls from it are handled with flood cleanup, water extraction and burst pipe response first. Scope and price are explained before work starts. For a number tied to your house, describe the damage to dispatch.

Your policy decides coverage. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, while rising water is usually excluded. Ask your insurer.

Before the next storm

Walk your property and look at how water moves. Clean gutters, extend downspouts and clear drainage swales. If you live near Cowskin Creek or a retention pond, keep valuables off basement floors and test your sump pump.

Learn the city's flood information and keep important papers in a waterproof container. A few quiet hours of preparation can save a stressful night.

A note on standards and safety: ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard crews follow for water damage. Floodwater is Category 3 under it, so do not wade through it, and call the utility or 911 for electrical risk.

Is my Haysville home in a floodplain?

Use the Kansas Floodplain Viewer or FEMA's Map Service Center for your address, and ask the City of Haysville about permits. Do not rely on a neighbor's answer.
